Chapter 1

About This System

RealEstateManager is a web-based system that manages a real-estate portfolio end to end: the properties you own or manage, the units inside them, the tenants who occupy them, the lease contracts that bind the two, and the complete accounting that results from all of it.

Its distinguishing characteristic is that operations and accounting are not two separate systems. Every operational action you take — signing a lease, receiving a cheque, issuing an invoice — produces a balanced double-entry journal automatically. You are never asked to write the accounting entry yourself, and the system refuses to save any journal whose debits do not equal its credits.

What the system covers

AreaWhat it does
PortfolioProperties (owned or managed), units within each property, occupancy status, standard rents and areas.
LeasingTenant register, lease contracts with billing frequency and VAT, automatic generation of the full invoice schedule and the monthly revenue-recognition plan.
CollectionsPost-dated cheque (PDC) register with memorandum and clearance postings, customer invoices, customer receipts, security deposits.
PayablesVendor register, vendor bills with expense categories and recoverable VAT, vendor payments.
Assets & deferralsFixed asset register with acquisition-cost accumulation, capitalisation and automatic depreciation schedules; recurring prepayment amortisation.
General ledgerFive-digit chart of accounts, account mapping, opening balances, manual journals, journal inquiry, monthly period close and fiscal year close.
ReportingTrial Balance, Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, General Ledger, Tenant Statement.
AdministrationMulti-company support, per-user permissions, subscription control, audit logging, user feedback.

Two ownership models

Every property is flagged as either Owned or Managed, and this single flag changes the accounting for every lease under it:

  • Owned — the rent is your revenue. Invoiced rent sits in Advances from Customers and is released monthly into Rental Revenue – Owned.
  • Managed — the rent belongs to the landlord. Invoiced rent sits in Due to Landlords, and only your commission is released into Management Fee Revenue.
Set the ownership type correctly the first time

Ownership type is read at the moment an invoice or a revenue-recognition entry is posted. Changing it after transactions exist will not restate what has already been posted.

How to use this manual

  • Menu ▸ Screen notation shows where to find each function, e.g. Master Data ▸ Properties.
  • Green/red Dr / Cr tables show the exact journal the system posts. These are reference, not data entry — the system writes them for you.
  • Chapter 3 is the map. If you read only one chapter, read that one.
  • Appendix B collects every automatic journal in one place; Appendix D is the troubleshooting guide.

If your user account has access to more than one company, a company selector appears at the top right. Choosing a different company reloads the current page in that company's data. If the company's subscription expires, a red banner appears and the Master Data, Accounts and Reports menus are withdrawn until an activation code is entered under Setup ▸ Subscription. Read-only access to Setup remains so that the subscription can be renewed.

  5. Day 5 — Cheques. Log the PDCs held against each lease.

Chapter 4

Chart of Accounts

Setup ▸ Chart of Accounts

The chart of accounts is the backbone of the ledger. A complete five-digit chart is created automatically for every new company, so in most cases you review it rather than build it.

4.1 How the codes are organised

The six codes above are group headers: they organise the tree and cannot be posted to. Detail accounts sit beneath them. The full seeded list is in Appendix A.

4.2 Reading the tree

Accounts are shown as an expandable tree with the account code, name, type, reporting group and current balance. Group rows are shown in bold and have no Edit action for posting purposes.

4.3 Adding an account

  1. Select New Account.
  2. Enter a Code that fits the range of its parent — a new expense account under General Expenses should begin with 6.
  3. Enter the Name, choose the Account Type and the Reporting Group. These two drive how the account behaves in the reports.
  4. Choose the Parent Account so the account appears in the right branch of the tree.
  5. Leave Is Postable ticked for a detail account; untick it (and tick Is Group) for a heading.
  6. Set the Opening Balance only if you are migrating from another system — see Chapter 6.
  7. Save.

For Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable and the fixed-asset accounts, the opening balance is calculated from the tenants, vendors and assets beneath them. Typing an opening balance directly onto those accounts has no effect — enter it on the tenant, vendor or asset instead.

4.4 Accounts you should not remove

Several accounts are referenced by the automatic postings. Deactivating one of them will cause the related transaction to fail with a mapping error. Before retiring any account, check Account Mapping (Chapter 5) to confirm it is not in use.

Chapter 5

Account Mapping

Setup ▸ Account Mapping

Account Mapping is the bridge between the operational side of the system and the ledger. When you post a customer receipt, the system needs to know which account is "cash" and which is "accounts receivable". Account Mapping answers exactly that question, once, for the whole company.

Defaults are applied automatically when a company is created, so the system works out of the box. You change a mapping only when your chart of accounts differs from the standard one — for example if you open a dedicated bank account and want receipts to land there instead of in 11100.

5.2 Changing a mapping

  1. Open Setup ▸ Account Mapping.
  2. Choose the replacement account from the dropdown beside the mapping you want to change.
  3. Save.
Mapping changes are not retrospective

Changing a mapping affects future postings only. Journals already posted keep the account they were posted to. If you re-point "Default Cash" to a new bank account mid-year, the earlier balance stays in the old account and must be moved with a manual journal.

If a posting fails with a mapping error

A message such as "Account mapping for Default Cash is not configured" means the mapping is empty or points at an account that has been deleted or deactivated. Open Account Mapping, select a valid account, save, and retry the posting.

Chapter 6

Opening Balances

Setup ▸ Opening Balances

Opening balances carry your position from a previous system into RealEstateManager. This screen is both the place where you verify them and the single most important control in the whole setup.

6.1 Where each opening balance is entered

BalanceEntered onScreen
Ordinary GL accounts (cash, equity, loans…)The account itselfSetup ▸ Chart of Accounts ▸ Edit
Accounts ReceivableEach tenantMaster Data ▸ Tenants ▸ Edit
Accounts PayableEach vendorMaster Data ▸ Vendors ▸ Edit
Fixed asset cost and accumulated depreciationEach assetAccounts ▸ Fixed Assets

The control accounts are deliberately not editable directly. Their opening balance is always the sum of the sub-ledger beneath them, which guarantees that the receivables ledger and the AR control account can never drift apart.

6.2 Reading the reconciliation screen

The screen shows three summary cards — Total Debit Opening Balance, Total Credit Opening Balance and Net Imbalance (Dr − Cr) — followed by a per-account table showing, for each account, the manual opening balance, the sub-ledger total, the effective opening balance actually used, and its debit/credit presentation. Beneath the table are the tenant, vendor and fixed-asset detail lists that make up the control account totals.

Net Imbalance must be 0.00

Opening balances are added into the Trial Balance and Balance Sheet as figures, not as a posted journal. Nothing forces the debit side to equal the credit side. If the Net Imbalance card shows any value other than zero, your Trial Balance will be out of balance by exactly that amount, and your Balance Sheet will not balance either.

The usual cause is entering asset opening balances (cash, receivables) without entering the matching credit-side opening balance on 31100 Share Capital or 32100 Retained Earnings – Prior Years.

6.3 Procedure for entering opening balances

  1. Obtain the closing trial balance from your previous system as at the day before you go live.
  2. Enter each tenant's outstanding balance on the tenant record. A positive value is an amount owed to you; a negative value is an advance you are holding.
  3. Enter each vendor's outstanding balance on the vendor record.
  4. Enter each fixed asset's opening cost and opening accumulated depreciation on the asset.
  5. Enter every other balance directly on its GL account (cash, bank, loans, share capital, retained earnings).
  6. Open Setup ▸ Opening Balances and confirm Net Imbalance = 0.00 with a green Balanced badge.
  7. Run Reports ▸ Trial Balance and confirm total debit equals total credit.
Balancing figure

If your legacy trial balance was itself balanced, the figure that makes the Net Imbalance zero is your retained earnings. Post it to 32100 Retained Earnings – Prior Years, not to a suspense account.

  3. Enter the Unit Number — unique within that property (e.g. 101, G-04).
  4. Enter the Floor and the Usage Type.
  5. Enter the Area (sq ft) and the Standard Annual Rent.
  6. Leave Status as Vacant — the system changes it automatically.
  7. Save.

Creating an active lease sets the unit to Occupied automatically. Set the status manually only for Under Maintenance or Reserved, which keep the unit out of the leasing flow.

8.2 Occupancy

The dashboard occupancy rate is derived from unit status, so keeping unit status accurate keeps the portfolio reporting accurate. A unit left as Vacant while actually leased understates occupancy.

Chapter 11

Lease Contracts

Master Data ▸ Leases  ·  Workflow step 4

The lease is the pivot of the whole system. Saving one lease creates, in a single action, the contract, the entire schedule of rent invoices for its term, and the month-by-month revenue-recognition plan.

11.1 Creating a lease

  1. Select New Lease.
  2. Choose the Unit. The list shows PROPERTY/UNIT.
  3. Choose the Tenant.
  4. Enter a unique Contract Number.
  5. Enter the Start Date and End Date.
  6. Enter the Annual Rent for the full contract year.
  7. Choose the Billing Frequency (Months) — must divide 12 exactly: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 or 12.
  8. Tick Is VAT Applicable and enter the VAT Rate if the rent is taxable.
  9. Enter the Security Deposit Amount agreed in the contract.
  10. Add any Notes, then Save.

11.2 What the system validates

RuleMessage you will see
End date after start date"End date must be later than start date."
Billing frequency divides 12"Billing frequency must be 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, or 12."
Contract number unique in the company"Contract number already exists."
No overlapping active lease on the same unit"This unit already has an overlapping active lease."

11.3 What is generated automatically

Invoice schedule. The annual rent is divided by the number of invoices per year (12 ÷ billing frequency). One draft invoice is created for each billing date from the start date until the end date, numbered INV-{contract}-{yyyyMM}. VAT is calculated per invoice and rounded to two decimals.

Revenue-recognition plan. One planned entry is created for the last day of every calendar month from the start month to the end month, each for one-twelfth of the annual rent. This is what spreads the rent evenly over time regardless of how it was billed or collected.

Unit status. The unit is set to Occupied and the lease status to Active.

Worked example

Annual rent 175,000, billed quarterly, one-year term starting 1 January:

  • 4 invoices of 43,750 each, dated 1 Jan, 1 Apr, 1 Jul, 1 Oct.
  • 12 revenue-recognition entries of 14,583.33 each, dated at each month end.

After eight months, 116,666.64 has been recognised as revenue and 58,333.36 remains in Advances from Customers as deferred income — which is exactly how the Trial Balance should look.

Editing a lease does not regenerate its schedules

Changing the annual rent, dates or billing frequency of an existing lease updates the contract only. The invoices and revenue-recognition entries already generated are left untouched. If commercial terms change materially, the clean approach is to terminate the lease and raise a new contract, then cancel any unposted invoices from the old one.

Chapter 12

PDC Receipts

Accounts ▸ PDC Receipts  ·  Workflow step 5

Post-dated cheques are the normal way rent is secured in the region. A PDC is a cheque you hold today that the bank will only honour on a future date. The system tracks it in two stages, each with its own journal, so the cheque is visible in the accounts from the day you receive it without overstating cash.

12.1 Stage one — receiving the cheque

  1. Open Accounts ▸ PDC Receipts and select New PDC.
  2. Choose the Lease. Only active leases are listed.
  3. Choose the Settlement Mode:
    • Receivable — the cheque pays a specific invoice. You must then select the invoice.
    • Advance — the cheque is taken before any invoice exists and will sit as a customer advance.
  4. If the mode is Receivable, choose the Invoice. Only invoices with an outstanding balance appear.
  5. Enter the Cheque Number (unique in the company) and the Bank Name.
  6. Enter the Cheque Date (the date written on the cheque) and the Received Date (the date you took possession).
  7. Enter the Amount and any Notes, then Save.

On save, the memorandum journal is posted immediately and the PDC status becomes Received:

AccountDebitCredit
11400 PDC On HandAmount—
21600 PDC Contra—Amount

This is a memorandum pair: it records custody of the cheque without touching cash or receivables. Its net effect on the balance sheet is nil.

12.2 Stage two — clearing the cheque

When the bank honours the cheque:

  1. Find the PDC in the list and select Clear.
  2. Enter the Cleared Date. It cannot be earlier than the received date.
  3. Confirm. The status becomes Cleared.

Clearance posts a single four-line journal that both reverses the memorandum and records the real money:

AccountDebitCredit
21600 PDC Contra (reverse memo)Amount—
11400 PDC On Hand (reverse memo)—Amount
11100 Cash and Cash EquivalentsAmount—
11200 Accounts Receivable (Receivable mode)
21200 Advances from Customers (Advance mode)		—Amount
Clearing is self-completing

If the memorandum was never posted, clearance posts it first. If the linked invoice was still in draft, clearance posts the invoice first so there is a receivable to settle. You do not need to sequence these by hand.

12.3 Validation rules

  • Cheque number must be unique within the company.
  • Amount must be greater than zero.
  • In Receivable mode an invoice is mandatory, it must belong to the selected lease, and the PDC amount cannot exceed the invoice's outstanding balance.
  • A PDC cannot be cleared twice.

12.4 PDC statuses

Chapter 13

Customer Invoices

Accounts ▸ Customer Invoices  ·  Workflow step 6

Rent invoices are created for you when the lease is saved (Chapter 11). Your job on this screen is to review them and post them when they fall due.

13.1 The invoice list

The list shows the invoice number, date, tenant, unit, subtotal, VAT, total, amount paid and status. Invoices generated from a lease begin in Draft: they exist as documents but have not touched the ledger.

13.2 Posting an invoice

  1. Locate the invoice in the list.
  2. Select Post.
  3. The journal below is written and the status becomes Posted.

Why invoicing does not create revenue

Billing and earning are different events. Issuing a rent invoice creates a receivable and an obligation to provide occupancy — not revenue. Revenue arrives month by month through revenue recognition (Chapter 16). This is what allows the Profit & Loss to show a smooth monthly rent figure even when the tenant is billed annually in advance.

13.3 Invoice statuses

Post invoices in the period they belong to

The journal takes the invoice date, not the date you press Post. Posting a January invoice in March still dates the journal in January — which is correct, but means the January reports change after you post. Post promptly, and never post into a closed period.

Chapter 14

Customer Receipts

Period & Year Closing

Setup ▸ Closing

Closing locks a period so that its reported figures cannot change afterwards. The screen shows the twelve monthly periods of the selected fiscal year with their status, and the history of fiscal years already closed.

23.1 Month-end checklist

  1. Post every customer invoice dated in the month.
  2. Record every customer receipt and clear every PDC that the bank honoured.
  3. Post every vendor bill and vendor payment.
  4. Run Revenue Recognition to the month-end date.
  5. Run Depreciation to the month-end date.
  6. Process Prepayment amortisation for the month.
  7. Post any accruals or adjustments as manual journals.
  8. Run the Trial Balance and confirm debits equal credits.
  9. Review the Profit & Loss and Balance Sheet.

23.2 Closing the fiscal year

After the final period of the year is closed and reviewed, use Close Year. The system posts a closing journal that transfers the net result of every revenue and expense account into equity:

Chapter 24

Trial Balance

Reports ▸ Trial Balance

The Trial Balance lists every account with a balance as at a chosen date and proves that the ledger is internally consistent. It is the first report to run and the last one to check.

24.1 Running the report

  1. Enter the As Of Date.
  2. Select Refresh.

The report is cumulative: it includes every posted journal from the beginning of the ledger up to and including the as-of date, plus opening balances. That is the correct behaviour for a trial balance — it shows a position, not a movement. To see movement between two dates, use the General Ledger report (Chapter 27), which shows opening balance, period debits, period credits and closing balance per account.

Chapter 25

Profit & Loss

Reports ▸ Profit & Loss

The Profit & Loss reports performance over a period: revenue earned less expenses incurred between two dates.

25.1 Running the report

  1. Enter the As Of Date — the end of the period you want.
  2. Select Refresh. The report covers 1 January of that year to the as-of date.

25.2 What it contains

SectionContents

Invoicing a tenant does not create revenue. If the Profit & Loss shows less rent than you expect, the usual reason is that revenue recognition has not been processed up to the report date. Run Setup ▸ Revenue Recognition and refresh.

Non-calendar fiscal years

The report currently starts the period at 1 January of the as-of year. The Profit & Loss by Property report (Chapter 28) does not have this limitation: it takes an explicit From and To date and defaults to the company's configured fiscal year. Use it when you need a period other than calendar year to date.

This report presents the company as a whole. To see the same revenue and expenses broken down per property, use Reports ▸ P&L by Property — see Chapter 28.

26.3 The balancing check

Total Assets must equal Total Liabilities and Equity. If they differ, the cause is almost always one of:

  • Unbalanced opening balances. Check Setup ▸ Opening Balances; the difference will match the Net Imbalance exactly.
  • An unclosed prior fiscal year. Prior-year profit remains in revenue and expense accounts, which the Balance Sheet does not present. Close the year (Chapter 23).
Cross-check against the Trial Balance

Trial Balance total debit minus total credit equals Total Assets minus Total Liabilities and Equity. The two reports fail together and are fixed together — you never have to diagnose them separately.

Management Reports

The three reports in this chapter answer management questions rather than accounting questions: which property is making money, how hard is the portfolio working, and which tenants can we rely on. They are listed under Reports ▸ Management Reports and require the View Reports permission.

28.1 Profit & Loss by Property

Reports ▸ P&L by Property

This is the general ledger Profit & Loss analysed across the property dimension that every journal line already carries. Accounts run down the rows, properties across the columns, so you can read one property's result down a column or one account across the whole portfolio along a row.

  2. Optionally choose a single Property to narrow the report to one column.
  3. Select Refresh.

Each column shows total revenue, total expenses, net profit or loss and net margin percentage. The final column totals the row across all properties and reconciles to the general ledger Profit & Loss for the same dates.

The Unallocated column

A journal line only carries a property if the source transaction identified one. Invoices, receipts, PDCs, revenue recognition and security deposits always do, because they descend from a lease. Vendor bills do when a property is chosen on the bill. Manual journals only do when a unit / cost centre is selected on the line — the property is then derived from that unit.

Anything without a property is collected into a highlighted Unallocated column so the report still reconciles to the GL. If that column is large, the fix is to reopen those manual journals and set the unit.

Revenue is presented as credits less debits and expenses as debits less credits, so a negative figure means a reversal or reclassification occurred in the period rather than an error.

28.3 Tenant Health & Ranking

Reports ▸ Tenant Health & Ranking

Every tenant scored from 0 to 100 and ranked best first, with the underlying figures shown alongside the score so the ranking can always be explained to the tenant or to management.

The four components

ComponentWeightBasis
Collection ratio40%Total collected ÷ total invoiced. The strongest single signal of a paying tenant. Collections include cleared PDCs, because clearance updates the invoice.
Overdue exposure30%Overdue balance ÷ total invoiced, inverted, then reduced by an ageing penalty of up to 30 points as the oldest overdue invoice passes 120 days.
PDC reliability15%Cleared cheque value ÷ (cleared + bounced) value.

A component that cannot be measured — a tenant who has never issued a post-dated cheque, or one with no invoices yet — is dropped, and its weight is shared across the components that can be measured. Nobody is penalised for the absence of a behaviour rather than for the behaviour itself. A tenant with no measurable activity at all is shown as No activity and graded N/A.

Columns worth knowing

  • Outstanding includes the tenant's opening balance, so it agrees with the tenant statement.
  • Overdue counts only posted invoices past their due date and not fully paid. Draft and cancelled invoices are excluded because they carry no ledger balance.
  • Oldest (days) is the age of the earliest unpaid overdue invoice — often more telling than the amount.
  • Avg days to pay is the average of receipt date less invoice due date. A negative figure means the tenant pays early.
  • PDC on hand is cheque value held but not yet cleared — future cash, not yet collected.

PDC reliability needs a bounce workflow to discriminate

The PDC register supports a Bounced status, but no screen currently sets it — a cheque is either Received or Cleared. Until a bounce action exists, the PDC reliability component scores 100% for every tenant who has had a cheque clear, and therefore does not yet separate good payers from bad ones. The other three components carry the ranking in the meantime.

Chapter 29

Owner Withdrawals

Money the owner takes out of the business for personal use is not a company expense. It is a distribution — a movement in equity. Putting it through the company's expense accounts would understate profit and misstate the Profit & Loss.

But the owner still wants to know what they spent it on. RealEstateManager solves this with a dedicated analysis dimension: the posting goes to a single equity account, and each line carries an owner withdrawal category that says what the money was for.

Why this is not a chart-of-accounts problem

Adding expense accounts for "school fees" or "personal travel" would put the owner's private life into the company's books and inflate its cost base. Adding them as unit cost centres would be worse still — cost centres in this system are units, used to allocate rental revenue and property costs to the space that generated them. Owner spending relates to no unit at all.

A separate dimension keeps the chart of accounts clean, keeps cost centres meaningful, and still gives the owner a full breakdown.

29.1 The owner's current account

The standard chart seeds one equity account for this purpose:

CodeAccountType
34100Owner's Current AccountEquity, flagged as an owner account

Because it is equity, a credit balance means the business owes the owner, and a withdrawal is a debit that reduces that balance. Capital the owner puts in is a credit.

If you have more than one owner, add one account per owner — 34200, 34300 and so on — under Setup ▸ Chart of Accounts, setting Owner Account to Yes on each. The same category list serves all of them, and each gets its own column in the report. Only a postable Equity account can be flagged this way; the setting is ignored on any other type.

Chapter 31

Subscription, Audit Log & Feedback

29.1 Subscription

Setup ▸ Subscription

Shows the company's current subscription state, its expiry date and the days remaining. New companies start on a 15-day trial. To extend, enter the activation code supplied by your provider and select Activate. When a subscription expires, processing menus are withdrawn until it is renewed; no data is lost.

29.2 Audit Logs

Setup ▸ Audit Logs — system administrators only.

Records who did what and when across the application. Filter by user, date range, action or entity to investigate a change. Combined with Journal Inquiry, this gives a complete evidence trail: the audit log shows the user action, the journal shows its accounting consequence.

29.3 Help

Setup ▸ Help

A built-in quick guide covering the main workflows, with links to this manual and to the interactive Workflow screen.

29.4 Feedback

Setup ▸ Feedback

Submit a problem report or an improvement request. Include the screen name, what you did, what you expected and what happened, plus any document number involved — that is usually enough to resolve an issue without further correspondence. Administrators review submissions under Setup ▸ Feedback Review.

Appendix D

Troubleshooting

D.1 The Trial Balance does not balance

Because the system rejects any unbalanced journal, transactions cannot cause this. The cause is opening balances.

  1. Open Setup ▸ Opening Balances.
  2. Read the Net Imbalance (Dr − Cr) card. It will equal the Trial Balance difference exactly.
  3. Identify the missing side. Debits exceeding credits means a credit-side opening balance is missing — almost always equity.
  4. Open Setup ▸ Chart of Accounts, edit 31100 Share Capital or 32100 Retained Earnings – Prior Years, and enter the balancing figure.
  5. Return to Opening Balances and confirm the green Balanced badge, then re-run the Trial Balance.
Worked diagnosis

Trial Balance shows debits 375,000 and credits 175,000 — a difference of 200,000. The credit side contains only transaction balances, and Share Capital and Retained Earnings are both nil. The conclusion is that 200,000 of asset opening balances were entered with no matching equity opening balance. Entering 200,000 on 31100 or 32100 balances both the Trial Balance and the Balance Sheet.

D.2 The Balance Sheet does not balance

  • Check opening balances as in D.1 — the same difference appears in both reports.
  • Check that every prior fiscal year has been closed. Unclosed prior-year profit sits in revenue and expense accounts, which the Balance Sheet does not present.

D.3 Revenue is missing from the Profit & Loss

  • Run Setup ▸ Revenue Recognition up to the report date. Invoicing alone does not create revenue.
  • Confirm the invoices themselves are Posted, not still in Draft.
  • For a managed property, remember only the commission is revenue — the rent belongs to the landlord.

D.4 A transaction will not post

MessageCause and fix
"Journal entry is not balanced."A manual journal line has an amount in both the debit and credit columns, or the totals differ. Correct the line.
"A journal entry must contain at least two lines."Add the counterpart line.
"Account mapping for … is not configured."Open Setup ▸ Account Mapping and select a valid, active account for that mapping.
"Contract number already exists."Contract numbers are unique per company. Use a different one.
"This unit already has an overlapping active lease."Terminate or adjust the existing lease first.
"Cheque number already exists."Cheque numbers are unique per company. Check whether the PDC was already logged.
"PDC amount cannot exceed the outstanding invoice balance."Reduce the amount, or split the cheque across invoices.
"Billing frequency must be 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, or 12."Billing frequency must divide 12 exactly.

D.5 A menu is not visible

  • Confirm the permission with your administrator (Chapter 30).
  • Check that the company's subscription has not expired — expiry withdraws the processing menus.
  • Confirm you are in the right company using the company selector.

D.6 A tenant's balance looks wrong

  1. Run Reports ▸ Tenant Statement for the tenant across the full period.
  2. Check for invoices still in Draft — they are not in the ledger.
  3. Check for PDCs still in Received status — the cash has not arrived yet.
  4. Check for a security deposit wrongly entered as a customer receipt.
  5. Confirm the tenant's opening balance was entered on the tenant record, not on the AR account.
Appendix E

Glossary

TermMeaning
Accrual basisRecording income when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred, regardless of when cash moves. The basis this system uses.
Advances from CustomersAccount 21200. Rent billed but not yet earned. A liability, released to revenue month by month.
Control accountA GL account whose balance is the total of a sub-ledger — Accounts Receivable over tenants, Accounts Payable over vendors.
Double entryEvery transaction affects at least two accounts with equal debits and credits, so the ledger is always self-proving.
Due to LandlordsAccount 21500. Rent collected on a managed property that belongs to its owner.
Fiscal yearThe company's twelve-month reporting year, starting in the configured fiscal year start month.
Managed propertyA property you administer for a third-party owner. Only your commission is revenue.
Natural balanceThe side an account normally sits on: debit for assets and expenses, credit for liabilities, equity and revenue.
Opening balanceA balance brought forward from a previous system at go-live.
Owned propertyA property you own. The rent is your revenue.
PDCPost-dated cheque — a cheque held today, payable on a future date.
PostingWriting a document's journal into the general ledger. Before posting, a document has no accounting effect.
Revenue recognitionReleasing billed rent into revenue as the occupancy is provided, one month at a time.
Sub-ledgerA detailed register behind a control account — the tenant register, the vendor register, the fixed asset register.
Trial balanceA listing of every account balance at a date, proving total debits equal total credits.
Unearned revenueSee Advances from Customers.
