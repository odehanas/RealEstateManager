A vendor bill records a cost you have incurred and the obligation to pay the supplier. Bills are entered the way Odoo does it: a header for the supplier and the dates, and as many lines as the supplier's document has, each charged to its own account and property.

Entering a bill

  1. Select New Bill and choose the Supplier. A coloured banner immediately tells you whether this supplier can charge VAT (see below).
  2. Enter the Supplier's Bill No. — the number printed on their invoice. Your own bill number, such as BILL-2026-0001, is issued when you save.
  3. Enter a Category / Subject to describe the bill on lists, e.g. Building maintenance.
  4. Enter the Bill Date (the date on their document), the Accounting Date and the Due Date.
  5. Optionally choose a Default Property. New lines pick it up automatically.
  6. Fill in the lines: description, account, property, quantity, unit price, and for a VAT-registered supplier whether the line is taxable and its input tax attribution. + Add line adds a row and × removes one. Totals update as you type.
  7. Add any Notes and select Save Draft.
  8. Review the bill, then select Post.
Bill date and accounting date

They are usually the same. They differ when a supplier's document arrives after its month has been closed: keep the supplier's date as the bill date, and set the accounting date in the open period. The accounting date decides which period the journal lands in.

VAT is decided by the supplier

SupplierWhat happens on the bill
VAT registered with a TRNBlue banner: Tax Invoice — 5% VAT applies. Taxable lines get 5% VAT, calculated for you.
Not registeredAmber banner: Commercial Invoice — no VAT. The VAT controls switch off, no VAT is calculated anywhere, and the full amount is charged to cost.

The supplier's registration is set on the vendor record — see Vendors. It is copied onto the bill when you save, so a later change to the vendor never alters a bill already entered.

Input tax attribution, line by line

How much of the VAT you can reclaim depends on what the cost was for. Set it on each line:

AttributionUse forResult
TaxableCosts of commercial (standard-rated) propertyVAT reclaimed in full.
ExemptCosts of residential lettingsNone reclaimed; the VAT is added to the cost of that line.
ResidualOverheads serving both, e.g. head officeSplit by the provisional recovery rate in VAT Settings, corrected at year end.

Because attribution is per line, one supplier invoice covering a commercial tower and a residential block is handled correctly: one line reclaims, the other does not.

VAT you cannot reclaim is never shown on its own line. It is part of what the item cost, so it is added to the line it belongs to.

Draft, post and reset

  • A draft bill can be edited freely and has no effect on the accounts.
  • Post builds the journal from the current lines and posts it, subject to the closed-period rules.
  • Reset to Draft takes it back out of the accounts so it can be edited, then posted again. This is refused if the period is closed or a payment has been posted against the bill.

See Draft, Post & Reset to Draft.

Printing

Print on the bill produces an A4 document with your letterhead, the supplier and bill details, the lines, the totals and a signature strip for Prepared by / Checked by / Approved by. If the supplier is not VAT registered, the printed copy says so.
