Workflow step 7. A customer receipt records money collected from a tenant — cash, bank transfer, or a cheque you are banking directly — and matches it to the invoices it pays. Cheques handled through the PDC register clear themselves and do not need a receipt.

Recording a receipt

  1. Select New Receipt, or Receive on the invoice in Customer Invoices.
  2. Choose the Tenant who paid. Pick from the list, or type part of the name, the tenant number or the phone number in Find the tenant; when only one tenant matches, it is chosen for you.
  3. The tenant's open invoices are listed, nearest due date first, each with its outstanding balance.
  4. Enter the Amount received and the Receipt Date — the date the money actually arrived.
  5. Tick the invoice or invoices the tenant is paying. This is optional: see Where the money goes below.
  6. Leave the Receipt Number blank to have one generated, or enter your own — it must be unique within the company.
  7. Choose the Payment Method, add the bank's reference if there is one, and select Save and Post.

Where the money goes

The table shows, as you type, what each invoice receives from this payment and what will be left to pay on it.

  • The invoices you ticked are paid first, nearest due date first.
  • If nothing is ticked, or money is left over, it goes to the remaining invoices, again nearest due date first.
  • An invoice that receives all it owes is Settled in full.
  • An invoice that receives only part is a Payment on account: the payment is accepted, the invoice is reduced, and the rest stays due. A tenant who owes 10,000 and pays 4,000 has 4,000 on account against that invoice and 6,000 still to pay.
  • An amount greater than everything the tenant owes on posted invoices is refused. Receive what is owed, or post the next invoice first.

The settlement document

After saving, the system opens the settlement document for the payment: who paid, how much, and each invoice it was matched to with the amount applied, what is left to pay, and whether it was settled in full or is a payment on account. Print it or save it as a PDF to give the tenant as their receipt. Open it again at any time by selecting the receipt number in the Customer Receipts list.

A payment matched to several invoices is one settlement with a receipt line for each invoice, numbered REC-…/1, REC-…/2, and each line has its own journal. The tenant statement and the journal describe a part payment as Payment on account against invoice …

Save and Post puts the receipt straight into the accounts

The invoice and the tenant's balance go down at once. If the posting is refused - for example the period is closed - the receipt is kept as a draft and a yellow message says why; post it from Operations ▸ Journal Inquiry once that is resolved. See Draft, Post & Reset to Draft.

Only posted invoices can be received against

The tenant's list shows posted invoices with a balance. A draft invoice is not yet a receivable, so it is not listed and no money can be received against it. Post the invoice first; the Receive button on the invoice list only appears once it is posted.

Managed property: where the money lands

For a tenant of a managed property the debit follows Who holds the rent on the property. Nothing is chosen on the receipt; the credit to Accounts Receivable is the same in every case.

Effect on the invoice

When the receipt's journal is posted, the amount is added to the invoice's Amount Paid and its status moves to PartiallyPaid or Paid. Resetting the journal to draft takes it back off again.

One payment, several invoices

A tenant who pays one amount covering three invoices is entered once. The system splits the amount over the invoices and keeps a receipt line for each, so the tenant statement stays accurate at invoice level.

If saving fails

If the posting cannot be prepared — a missing account mapping, or a date in a closed period — nothing is saved and the message tells you why. You will not be left with a half-recorded receipt.
