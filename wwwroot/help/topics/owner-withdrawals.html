Money the owner takes out of the business for personal use is not a company expense. It is a distribution — a movement in equity. Putting it through the company's expense accounts would understate profit and misstate the Profit & Loss.

But the owner still wants to know what they spent it on. RealEstateManager solves this with a dedicated analysis dimension: the posting goes to a single equity account, and each line carries an owner withdrawal category that says what the money was for.

Why this is not a chart-of-accounts problem

Adding expense accounts for "school fees" or "personal travel" would put the owner's private life into the company's books and inflate its cost base. Adding them as unit cost centres would be worse still — cost centres in this system are units, used to allocate rental revenue and property costs to the space that generated them. Owner spending relates to no unit at all.

A separate dimension keeps the chart of accounts clean, keeps cost centres meaningful, and still gives the owner a full breakdown.

The owner's current account

The standard chart seeds one equity account for this purpose:

CodeAccountType
34100Owner's Current AccountEquity, flagged as an owner account

Because it is equity, a credit balance means the business owes the owner, and a withdrawal is a debit that reduces that balance. Capital the owner puts in is a credit.

If you have more than one owner, add one account per owner — 34200, 34300 and so on — under System Setup ▸ Chart of Accounts, setting Owner Account to Yes on each. The same category list serves all of them, and each gets its own column in the report. Only a postable Equity account can be flagged this way; the setting is ignored on any other type.

Posting owner expenditure

Operations ▸ Manual Journals ▸ New Manual Journal

The General Journal has an Owner Withdrawal Category column. It activates only on a line posted to an account flagged as an owner account; on every other line it stays locked and empty, and the row is highlighted when the dimension becomes available.

  1. Enter the Entry Date and a clear Description.
  2. On the first line choose the owner's current account and enter the amount as a Debit.
  3. Choose the Owner Withdrawal Category — the column is now enabled on that line.
  4. On the second line credit whatever the money came out of, normally 11100 Cash and Cash Equivalents.
  5. Select Save as Draft, then post it from Journal Inquiry.

Leave the category blank on money coming in. Categories describe expenditure; a contribution is not expenditure.

The category is refused on any other account

If you try to save a category against an account that is not flagged as an owner account, the journal is rejected with a message naming the offending account. This is enforced on the server, not just hidden in the screen — it is what guarantees personal spending can never reach a company account by accident.

Clearing the balance at year end

The owner's current account carries forward like any equity account. If you want to clear it against retained earnings — for example when drawings are formally treated as a distribution of profit — post a manual journal debiting 32100 Retained Earnings – Prior Years and crediting 34100 Owner's Current Account. Leave the category blank: it is a transfer, not expenditure.

The categories themselves are maintained under Owner Withdrawal Categories, and the spending is reported in Owner Reports.
