Reporting ▸ Building View, or Building view on a property's page.

Each building drawn floor by floor, every unit coloured by the health of its rent, so an owner sees at a glance where the building earns well and where it needs attention. Select a unit for its details.

All buildings

The first page shows every property as a card: how much of it is let, a bar of its units by colour, the counts of healthy, watch and at-risk units, and the rent overdue. Open building draws it.

The colours

ColourWhen
Green - healthyLet, and none of the warning signs below
Yellow - watchTenant health score below 75, or rent overdue more than 30 days, or a request to postpone a payment in the last 12 months
Red - at riskTenant health score below 55, or rent overdue more than 90 days, or a cheque on the contract bounced in the last 12 months
GreyVacant (no current contract); striped grey is under maintenance
BlueReserved for a buyer (light) or sold and handed over (darker) - see Unit Sales

The score is the tenant's score from Tenant Health & Ranking, so the drawing and the report always agree. Select a colour above the drawing to show only those units; select it again to show all.

A unit's details

Selecting a unit shows its tenant (linked to their statement) and contract, the health score and grade, the annual rent, what was billed, collected, outstanding and overdue (with the age of the oldest overdue item), bounced cheques and postponement requests in the last 12 months, the days to the contract's end (highlighted inside 90 days), a bar of billed rent collected, and Why this colour - each reason that set it. For a vacant unit it shows how long it has been empty and the rent lost.

How the building is drawn

  • Floors run from the lowest to the highest unit, and up to the property's Number of floors when recorded, so a tower with only some units entered still has its full height. Floors with no units say so.
  • Each unit is placed on its floor by the unit's Floor number; when that is empty, it is read from the Floor text (G = ground, 3 = third, B1 = first basement).
  • Along the floor, units go left to right by Position on the floor, then by unit number, and each is as wide as its share of the floor's area.

Set both on the unit in Master Data ▸ Units ▸ Edit to arrange a building exactly as it stands.

2.5D, 3D and Photo

The 2.5D | 3D | Photo switch above the drawing changes how the building is shown; the colours, the legend and the unit details are the same in both, and the choice is remembered on your computer.

  • 2.5D draws the building's front face with its side and roof at an angle. Every unit is visible at once, and it prints well.
  • 3D builds a model you can turn: drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag to move it. It turns slowly by itself until you touch it. Point at a unit to see its number; select it for its details.

In 3D each unit sits on its Side of the building - front, back, left or right - set on the unit. Front and back units share the depth of the floor; left and right units take its two ends. A floor whose units have no side recorded is shared between front and back.

Photo mode: the real building

In Photo mode the building view shows a real photograph of the building, with each unit's windows or balcony tinted in its colour. Select a tinted unit for its details; the legend filter works here too. A building can have several photos - front, back, sides - shown as buttons above the photo. Units not marked on the photo shown are listed underneath it.

Setting it up takes two steps, once per photo (needs Create & Edit Master Data):

  1. Upload the photo. On the building view select Manage photos and mark units (or Photo mode ▸ the upload link). Name the side it shows and choose the file - JPG or PNG, up to 8 MB. A photo taken straight on, with the whole building in frame, works best.
  2. Mark the units. The marking screen opens: pick a unit on the right, click the corners of its windows or balcony on the photo, and select Save outline (or press Enter). The next unmarked unit is chosen for you. Undo corner (Backspace) removes the last click; click a saved outline to select its unit again; Remove outline clears it.
A tower in minutes: repeat floors

Mark the units of two floors one above the other - for example floors 1 and 2. Under Repeat Floors Up the Building choose those two floors and the last floor, and select Repeat floors. The distance between the two marked floors is measured and the upper floor's outlines are repeated on every floor above, matched to each floor's units left to right (by their position on the floor, then unit number). Outlines already marked are kept unless you tick Replace. Check the result - a photo taken at an angle makes upper floors smaller - and adjust any outline that does not sit right.

Photos are stored with the company's data and seen only by users who can open the building view.
