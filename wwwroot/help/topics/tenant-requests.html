Everything tenants raise through the self-service portal lands here: maintenance calls, requests for more time to pay, contract renewal questions, complaints and anything else. You need the Handle Tenant Requests permission to see this screen.

Maintenance requests go to the maintenance team

A new maintenance request is assigned automatically to a user who carries out maintenance jobs — the least busy one — and appears in their My Maintenance Jobs list. Requests for more time to pay, complaints and other requests stay with you here. See Maintenance Jobs.

Maintenance is on hold for tenants with payment issues

A tenant who has an overdue invoice, or a returned (bounced) cheque that has not been replaced, cannot request maintenance through the portal. The New Request page tells them their maintenance requests are on hold, how much is overdue, and to contact the administration office. They can still send other requests — for example, asking for more time to pay.

  • An invoice is overdue when it is posted, past its due date and not fully paid. An invoice that a post-dated cheque on hand is waiting to settle does not count.
  • The hold lifts by itself as soon as the overdue amount is received or the cheque is replaced.
  • Staff can still raise or handle a request for the tenant when the office agrees to it.

The queue

The screen opens on the Open requests — the work still outstanding. The buttons across the top filter it: New (not yet acknowledged), In progress, Closed and All, each with a count. The Type filter narrows it further.

  • Requests are listed worst first: Urgent, then High, then oldest first.
  • The Age column turns red once a request is 7 days old.
  • An amber badge marks a payment postponement that was agreed but not yet applied to the invoice.

Working a request

  1. Select Open on the request. You see what the tenant asked, their contact details, the property, unit and contract, and the full conversation.
  2. Under Update, set the status to Acknowledged so the tenant knows it has been seen. Adjust the Priority if the tenant's own rating was off, and choose who it is Assigned to.
  3. Use the Conversation box to reply. The tenant sees your reply in their portal.
  4. Move it to In progress when work starts.
  5. When done, set Resolved and write the Response to the tenant — what was done. It is required, because the tenant reads it. Use Declined if you will not do it, again with the reason.
  6. Set Closed once nothing further is expected.
Internal notes

Tick Internal note before posting a message to keep it among staff — for instance, a note about the tenant's payment history. Internal notes are shown with an amber background and are never sent to the portal. Anything not ticked is visible to the tenant.

Payment postponement requests

A tenant asking for more time names the invoice and the date they could pay by. Agreeing to it and actually moving the date are two separate, deliberate steps:

  1. Decide, and set the request to Resolved with your response — for example "Agreed, invoice INV-… may be paid by 20 November."
  2. A button appears: Apply new due date to INV-…. Press it and confirm.

Only the invoice's due date changes. The amount owed, the VAT and the journal are untouched — giving more time to pay is not a credit note, so no figure in any financial report moves. The change is recorded in the conversation, the tenant sees it, and the button cannot be pressed twice.

What the tenant can and cannot do

  • Raise a request and add messages to it until it is closed.
  • Withdraw it, but only while it is Submitted or Acknowledged — once work is under way, it stays.
  • They cannot change its status, priority or assignment, and cannot see internal notes.

Requests that arrive by WhatsApp, phone or a visit

Not every tenant uses the portal. Choose Log a Request for a Tenant, find the tenant by name, code or phone number, say how the request reached you and when, and paste their message. It becomes an ordinary request on the tenant's record: the tenant sees it on the portal, a maintenance job goes to the maintenance team, and it counts on the dashboard, where its response time runs from the moment it came in. The thread records who logged it.

When the message comes in on a connected WhatsApp number, nobody needs to type it: see below.

Messages that arrive by themselves on WhatsApp

A company can connect its own WhatsApp Business number. A tenant then simply sends a message, and it becomes a request on their record: it shows in this queue with WhatsApp as its source, and on the tenant's portal. The people who handle requests are notified.

  • How the tenant is found. The sender's number is compared with the Phone on each active tenant. It must be the same number once written in full: +971 50 123 4567 and 050 123 4567 match, and a record may hold two numbers separated by a slash.
  • It arrives as "Other request". A message does not say what kind of request it is. Open it and use Sort this WhatsApp request. Choosing maintenance sends it to the maintenance team. You see any payment hold first, because a message from WhatsApp never gets round the hold by itself. For more time to pay, log a request yourself, because that needs an invoice and a date.
  • Several messages in a row. Further messages from the same person within 30 minutes join the request they just opened as messages in its conversation, while it is still open.
  • Sent twice by WhatsApp. WhatsApp sometimes repeats a delivery. Each message is only ever taken once.

The WhatsApp Messages review list

A message that cannot safely go on one tenant's record waits under WhatsApp Messages, and never reaches any tenant by itself. That happens when:

  • no active tenant has that number, or several do (for example a couple, or one company renting two units);
  • WhatsApp did not give the number, because the person uses a WhatsApp username;
  • the message has no text, such as a voice note or a photo with no caption;
  • the company's subscription was inactive when it arrived, or the request could not be created.

For each message, choose Log for a suggested tenant, Choose a tenant, or Dismiss it. Logging opens the usual form filled in with the message, and the time received is when it was sent. If the number really is that tenant's, add it to their record so the next message finds them by itself.

Nothing is sent back on WhatsApp

The system only receives. The tenant gets no reply on WhatsApp: your replies appear on the portal. Photos and voice notes are not stored here. A photo's caption becomes the request and the picture stays in WhatsApp, so ask for it if you need it.

Connecting a WhatsApp number

You need the Owner Oversight permission, a Meta Business account and a phone number that is not already in use on the ordinary WhatsApp apps. Meta may charge for some conversations, according to its own price list.

  1. In Meta for Developers, create an app with the WhatsApp product and add your number.
  2. In this system, open WhatsApp Messages, then WhatsApp Connection. Enter the Phone Number ID (under WhatsApp, API Setup) and the App Secret (under App settings, Basic), and save. The secret is stored encrypted and is never shown again.
  3. The screen now shows a Callback URL and a Verify token. Enter both in your Meta app under WhatsApp, Configuration, and subscribe to the messages field.
  4. Send a message to the number from a phone that is on a tenant's record, and check that it appears in the queue.
