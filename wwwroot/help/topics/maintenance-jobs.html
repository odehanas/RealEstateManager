Maintenance requests raised by tenants are worked as jobs by the maintenance team. Each job records who did it, when it was answered, started and finished, and the materials it used. The owner follows the whole operation on the Maintenance Dashboard.

Setting up the maintenance team

  1. Open System Setup ▸ Users & Permissions and select New User for the maintenance engineer or technician.
  2. Under Role, select Maintenance Engineer / Technician. This ticks only the permission Carry Out Maintenance Jobs.
  3. Grant access to the company and Save.

From then on, every new maintenance request from a tenant is assigned to a maintenance user automatically. With more than one, the job goes to the one with the fewest open jobs. If nobody has the permission yet, jobs wait unassigned and the Maintenance Jobs screen says so.

The maintenance manager

Give the person who runs maintenance the Maintenance Manager role, which ticks Manage Maintenance. They see every maintenance job, assign and close them, raise make-ready and inspection jobs, set up the Planned Maintenance programme and open the Maintenance Dashboard. New maintenance requests and low ratings reach them too. They do not see the other tenant requests or the accounts. The role also ticks Carry Out Maintenance Jobs, so jobs can be given to them; untick it if they only supervise.

What the maintenance user sees

Operations ▸ Tenant Services ▸ My Maintenance Jobs

A maintenance user sees only the jobs assigned to them — not the accounts, not other requests, and not other technicians' jobs. The list opens on the Open jobs, worst first (Urgent, then High, then oldest); Done and All show the rest. The age turns red once a job is past its target time.

A notification for every new job

A technician who installs the app on their phone and turns on notifications (the card on My Maintenance Jobs) is told the moment a job is given to them, and when the tenant replies on it. Tapping the notification opens the job.

Working a job

  1. Open the job. The card shows what the tenant asked, the unit, the tenant's phone number, the preferred visit date and the timeline.
  2. Select Acknowledge — I have seen it. This is the first response the dashboard measures.
  3. Select Start work when you begin. (Starting also counts as the first response if you skipped acknowledging.)
  4. Record the materials used, as below, and add messages as you go. Notes are internal by default; untick the box for a message the tenant should read.
  5. Write What was done and select Mark as done. The tenant reads this text in the portal.

A supervisor — anyone with Handle Tenant Requests — sees every job, can Assign it to another technician, Reopen it, and Close the job once nothing more is expected. A technician cannot close a job.

The maintenance store is a control account, not an inventory system

Buy stock for the store with a vendor bill (or petty cash) charged to account 11650. Items taken out for jobs reduce it. The balance is shown on the Maintenance Dashboard; a negative balance means items were issued before their purchase was booked.

Jobs no tenant asked for

Maintenance is not only what tenants ask for. A supervisor, anyone with Handle Tenant Requests, can raise a job from Maintenance Jobs ▸ Raise a Job: choose the property, and the unit or leave it for the building and its common areas, then say what it is for. The choices are making a vacant unit ready for the next tenant, a repair, an inspection, preventive maintenance or an improvement.

  • It goes to the least busy technician, or to the one you choose, and is worked like any other job: acknowledged, started, materials recorded and marked as done.
  • Materials are charged the same way: to the owner for a managed property, to the company for its own.
  • It has no tenant, so it never shows on a tenant's portal, all its notes are internal and it is not rated.
  • It is listed on Maintenance Jobs, not on Tenant Requests.

Units to make ready

When a tenancy ends, the unit should be inspected, repaired and cleaned before it is let again. Maintenance Jobs lists every vacant unit whose last tenancy has ended and that has had no make-ready job since, with a Raise make-ready job button. The owner sees the same list on the dashboard, with the days each unit has been vacant.

Planned maintenance

Regular work is set up once under Operations ▸ Tenant Services ▸ Planned Maintenance. Examples are the AC service every 3 months, the fire alarm check every 6 months and the water tank cleaning every 12.

  1. Select New Planned Work. Name the work, choose the property and, if it is for one unit, the unit.
  2. Say every how many months it is due, the next due date, and how many days before the due date the job should open, so the visit can be booked.
  3. Leave Assign to empty to give each job to the least busy technician, or choose the one who always does it.

The system checks every few hours. When a plan comes due it opens a job, marked Planned maintenance with its due date, and moves the plan to its next date. Open due jobs now runs the check at once. A planned job counts as late only once its due date has passed, and the dashboard shows the share of planned work done by its due date.

The Maintenance Dashboard

Controls ▸ Maintenance Dashboard — for the owner (Owner Oversight), supervisors and report viewers.

Choose a From and To date (the last 90 days by default) and, if you like, one technician. It covers the jobs raised in the period and every job still open:

  • Jobs received, how many are done and declined.
  • Still open, how many are past their target and how many are unassigned.
  • Average first response — from the request to the engineer acknowledging it.
  • Average time to complete, and the share done within target.
  • Materials used and the maintenance store balance.
  • The open jobs, worst first, and breakdowns by technician, by property and by kind of job.

Cost per property and per unit

The cost shown is the materials recorded on the jobs — items taken from the maintenance store and items bought for the job (see Materials above). The tiles give the total, how much came from the store and how much was bought, the materials per completed job, and how much was charged to owners. Jobs and cost by unit lists each unit with its jobs, its materials and the materials per job, highest cost first; work on a building's common areas is shown on its own line. A contractor's bill that was not recorded as a material on a job is not in these figures: it is in the property's costs under P&L by Property.

Excel and PDF

  • Export to Excel downloads a workbook for the same period and technician: a summary of the figures, then one sheet each for every job, cost by property, cost by unit, the technicians, the kinds of job, the months, and every material line with its quantity, cost and who it was charged to.
  • Print / Save as PDF lays the dashboard out as a Maintenance Report on A4, with the company name, the period and the date it was prepared. Choose Save as PDF in the print window to keep it as a file for the follow-up meeting or to send to an owner.

The tenant's rating

When a job is marked as done, the tenant is invited to rate it on the portal from 1 to 10 and write feedback. A job is rated once. If the tenant gives the rating by WhatsApp or phone, a supervisor can record it on the job; the job then shows who entered it, and nobody can enter the rating of a job they carried out themselves. Once a job has a rating it can no longer be moved to another technician.

The dashboard shows the team rating, which is the maintenance manager's score, as the average of all rated jobs, with each technician's own average beside their name, the number of low ratings, and the feedback, lowest first. A rating of 5 or below also sends a notification to the people who handle tenant requests.
