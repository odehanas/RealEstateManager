Closing locks a period so that its reported figures cannot change afterwards. The screen shows the twelve monthly periods of the selected fiscal year with their status, and the history of fiscal years already closed.

Month-end checklist

  1. Post every customer invoice dated in the month.
  2. Record every customer receipt and clear every PDC that the bank honoured.
  3. Post every vendor bill and vendor payment.
  4. Run Revenue Recognition to the month-end date.
  5. Run Depreciation to the month-end date.
  6. Process Prepayment amortisation for the month.
  7. Post any accruals or adjustments as manual journals.
  8. In Operations ▸ Journal Inquiry, set Posted Only to No for the month and Post All Drafts. Nothing left in draft is in the figures you are about to lock.
  9. Run the Trial Balance and confirm debits equal credits.
  10. Review the Profit & Loss and Balance Sheet.
  11. Return to System Setup ▸ Period Closing and Close Period.
Closing locks the period both ways

After a period is closed, nothing can be posted into it and nothing in it can be reset to draft. Late documents go into the next open period — for vendor bills, use the Accounting Date. Corrections are made with a journal in an open period.

Closing the fiscal year

After the final period of the year is closed and reviewed, use Close Year. The system posts a closing journal that transfers the net result of every revenue and expense account into equity:

The closing is recorded with its fiscal year, the profit or loss amount, the timestamp and a link to the journal, so it is fully auditable.

Close each year before reporting on the next

The Balance Sheet presents the current fiscal year's profit as a separate equity line and takes prior years from Retained Earnings. If a year is never closed, its profit stays in the revenue and expense accounts — which the Balance Sheet does not present — and the Balance Sheet will not balance once you cross into the following year.
