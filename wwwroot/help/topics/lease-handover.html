A handover is what happens when a contract ends and the tenant gives the unit back. Three people have a say, in order, and the system keeps each one's part:

  1. The inspection: the unit is checked against the company's checklist.
  2. The Maintenance Manager: prices anything the tenant must pay for, or signs that there is nothing.
  3. The accounts: check what the tenant still owes and settle the security deposit. The deposit pays what is owed and the rest is refunded.

Settling closes the contract, makes the unit vacant and produces the Unit Handover Report for signature. Nothing reaches the accounts before the settlement.

The checklist

Open Master Data ▸ Lease Handovers ▸ Checklist. The system starts with a standard list (keys, meter readings, walls and paint, floors, doors, kitchen, bathrooms, air conditioning, lights, cleaning). Add your own lines, rename them, move them up or down, or switch off the ones you do not use. Each new handover takes a copy of the lines switched on; a handover already opened keeps the checklist it started with.

Step 1: open the handover and inspect

  1. Open Lease Handovers, choose the contract and the handover date, and press Open. A date before the contract's end date makes it an early termination.
  2. For each checklist line choose OK, Problem found or Not applicable. A line marked as a problem needs a note saying what was found.
  3. Enter the keys returned and the electricity and water meter readings.
  4. Save, continue later keeps what is entered. Complete the inspection signs it off with your name and the time; it is refused while a line is still unchecked.

Step 2: the Maintenance Manager's charges

After the inspection, a user with Manage Maintenance opens the handover and, for each thing the tenant must pay for, enters what it is for and the amount before VAT (for example repainting a room damaged beyond fair wear, or a missing access card). Then Sign and pass to the accounts. With no charges entered, signing records that nothing is charged. Until the accounts settle, Reopen to correct the charges takes the signature back.

Step 3: the accounts settle the deposit

The box also warns about things to deal with first: a deposit or an invoice whose entry is not posted, invoices dated after the handover (rent for a period the tenant will not be there, to cancel or credit), cheques of the contract still in hand, and requests still open. These warnings do not stop the settlement.

A user with Manage Accounting presses Settle and close the contract. The system then:

  1. Raises a tax invoice to the tenant for the charges, numbered HNC-, credited to Other Income.
  2. Applies the deposit to each invoice owed, oldest first, as a receipt paid from the deposit.
  3. Posts the refund of what is left.
  4. Marks the deposit as applied or refunded, the contract as Expired (or Terminated for an early termination) and the unit as Vacant.

If one of these entries is left as a draft (for example the period is closed), the settlement stops and says why. Post the entry from Journal Inquiry and press Settle again: it carries on from where it stopped and does nothing twice.

The report

Handover report opens a page to print or save as PDF: the contract, the checklist with its results and notes, the keys and meter readings, the charges, the deposit settlement, and four signature boxes for the tenant, the inspector, the Maintenance Manager and the accounts. Before the settlement it is marked In progress and shows the figures as they stand that day.

Points to agree with your accountant

The charges are invoiced with VAT where the company is registered, and are credited to the company's Other Income, also for an owner's building. If your company treats damage recoveries differently, or passes them to the owner, tell your service provider so the handover follows your practice.

Who can do what

Users who manage leases, tenant requests, maintenance or accounting can open a handover and record the inspection. Only Manage Maintenance enters and signs the charges. Only Manage Accounting settles. The owner can look at handovers and their reports.
