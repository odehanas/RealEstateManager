The chart of accounts is the backbone of the ledger. A complete five-digit chart is created automatically for every new company, so in most cases you review it rather than build it.

How the codes are organised

RangeGroupNature
10000AssetsDebit
20000LiabilitiesCredit
30000EquityCredit
40000RevenueCredit
50000Direct ExpensesDebit
60000General ExpensesDebit

The six codes above are group headers: they organise the tree and cannot be posted to. Detail accounts sit beneath them. The full seeded list is in Chart of Accounts Reference.

Reading the tree

Accounts are shown as an expandable tree with the account code, name, type, reporting group and current balance. Group rows are shown in bold and have no Edit action for posting purposes.

Adding an account

  1. Select New Account.
  2. Enter a Code that fits the range of its parent — a new expense account under General Expenses should begin with 6.
  3. Enter the Name, choose the Account Type and the Reporting Group. These two drive how the account behaves in the reports.
  4. Choose the Parent Account so the account appears in the right branch of the tree.
  5. Leave Is Postable ticked for a detail account; untick it (and tick Is Group) for a heading.
  6. Set the Opening Balance only if you are migrating from another system — see Opening Balances.
  7. Save.

Opening BalanceManual opening balance in natural form: positive means a debit balance for asset/expense accounts and a credit balance for liability/equity/revenue accounts.
Control accounts take their opening balance from the sub-ledger

For Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable and the fixed-asset accounts, the opening balance is calculated from the tenants, vendors and assets beneath them. Typing an opening balance directly onto those accounts has no effect — enter it on the tenant, vendor or asset instead.

The opening balance check

The top of the screen totals every account's opening balance and checks the accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

  • A green banner means the opening balances agree.
  • A red banner means they do not, and shows the difference. Your Trial Balance and Balance Sheet will be out by exactly that amount until it is fixed — see Opening Balances.

Accounts you should not remove

Several accounts are referenced by the automatic postings. Deactivating one of them will cause the related transaction to fail with a mapping error. Before retiring any account, check Account Mapping (Account Mapping) to confirm it is not in use.
