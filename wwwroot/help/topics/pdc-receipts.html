Workflow step 5. Post-dated cheques are the normal way rent is secured in the region. A PDC is a cheque you hold today that the bank will only honour on a future date. The system follows it from your drawer to the bank and on to clearance — or back to the tenant if it bounces or is withdrawn — with a journal at each step, so the cheque is visible in the accounts from the day you receive it without overstating cash.

Two dates, two different jobs

The Received Date is when the cheque came into your hands. The Cheque Date is the date written on it. The accounts record the cheque on the received date — that is when you hold it — not on the cheque date. The cheque date only matters when the cheque is banked.

Stage one — receiving the cheque

  1. Select New PDC and choose the Lease. Only active leases are listed.
  2. Choose the Settlement Mode:
    • Receivable — the cheque pays a specific invoice, which you then select. Only invoices with an outstanding balance appear.
    • Advance — the cheque is taken before any invoice exists and will sit as a customer advance.
  3. Enter the Cheque Number (unique in the company) and the Bank Name.
  4. Enter the Received Date — it cannot be in the future — and the Cheque Date, which cannot be earlier than the received date.
  5. Enter the Amount and any Notes, then save.

Saving creates the memorandum journal, dated on the received date, as a draft. Post it from Operations ▸ Journal Inquiry; the cheque then shows as Received.

A memorandum pair: it records custody of the cheque without touching cash or receivables. Its net effect on the balance sheet is nil.

Correcting a cheque's details

Edit on a cheque lets you correct the received date, cheque date, cheque number, bank and notes — the usual fix when a cheque was keyed with the wrong date. Changing the received date re-dates the memorandum journal to match. The amount, lease and settlement mode cannot be changed here; if those are wrong, reset the journal and record the cheque again.

Stage two — depositing the cheque to the bank

When cheques fall due, they go to the bank for collection. Select Deposit on a cheque, or tick several on the register, choose the date and select Deposit to Bank (Tick cheques due by that date ticks them for you). The cheque shows as At bank.

Depositing reverses the memorandum and credits the tenant: in Receivable mode the invoice is settled now, and the cheque appears on the tenant's statement as a payment on the deposit date.

Stage three — clearing the cheque

  1. When the bank honours the cheque, select Clear (Cleared on a cheque at the bank).
  2. Enter the Cleared Date. It cannot be earlier than the received date.
  3. If the cleared date is before the cheque date, you are warned that a bank will not normally honour a post-dated cheque early, and asked to confirm. Confirm only if the cheque really was presented early by agreement.
  4. Save, then post the clearance journal from Journal Inquiry. The cheque becomes Cleared.

When the bank returns a cheque — Bounced

  1. On a cheque at the bank, select Bounced.
  2. Enter the date it was returned and the bank's reason (insufficient funds, signature mismatch...).
  3. The charge to the tenant starts at the company's returned cheque charge (0 for none); change it if needed.
  4. Select Record Bounce and Post.

The amount is charged back to the tenant: in Receivable mode the invoice is open again; in Advance mode the advance is taken back. The tenant statement shows the deposit and then the PDC Returned line.

A cheque recorded as bounced without being deposited here only has its memorandum reversed — the tenant was never credited, so there is nothing to charge back.

A bounced cheque holds the tenant

Until it is replaced, the tenant counts as having a returned cheque: their maintenance requests are held and it shows on Tenant Health and the building view. On the bounced cheque, select New cheque to receive the replacement (it starts with the same lease, invoice and amount, and the old cheque becomes Replaced), or Paid otherwise when the tenant settled by cash or transfer.

When the tenant asks for a cheque back — Withdraw

A tenant may ask to take back a cheque you hold, to pay another way or swap it. That costs the business, so the tenant is charged.

  1. On a cheque on hand, select Withdraw. (A cheque already at the bank cannot be withdrawn; if the bank returns it, record it as bounced.)
  2. Enter the date it was handed back and a note.
  3. The charge to the tenant starts at the company's withdrawal charge (500 by default); type a different amount if needed, or 0 for none. The screen shows the VAT and total.
  4. Select Withdraw and Post.

The memorandum is reversed and the cheque stays in the register as Withdrawn, with its charge. The rent it was for is still owed: in Receivable mode the invoice stays open. Replace it with New cheque or Paid otherwise, as for a bounced cheque.

Charges to the tenant

A withdrawal or returned cheque charge is raised as its own tax invoice to the tenant (numbered CHG-yyyy-nnnnn, described as Cheque withdrawal charge or Returned cheque charge), with 5% VAT when the company is VAT registered. It is on the tenant's statement, is collected like any invoice, and is declared on the company's VAT return as a standard-rated supply.

AccountDebitCredit
11200 Accounts ReceivableAmount—
43100 Other Income (or the mapped cheque charges income)
21400 VAT Payable		—Amount

The charge is always the company's own income, issued in its own name, even for a managed property whose rent is billed in the owner's name — it is never part of what the owner is paid. The default amounts and the income account are set in System Setup ▸ Account Mappings ▸ PDC Setup. To undo a withdrawal or bounce that carried a charge, credit the charge invoice first.

The invoice must be posted before the cheque clears

In Receivable mode, clearing settles the invoice, so the invoice has to be in the accounts already. If it is still a draft, clearing is refused with "Invoice … has not been posted yet". Post the invoice, then clear the cheque. You can still receive a cheque against a draft invoice — the memorandum entry does not touch the receivable.

Validation rules

  • Cheque number must be unique within the company.
  • Amount must be greater than zero.
  • In Receivable mode an invoice is required, it must belong to the selected lease, and the cheque cannot exceed the invoice's outstanding balance.
  • A cheque cannot be cleared twice, and a bounced, withdrawn or replaced cheque cannot be cleared.
  • Only a cheque on hand can be deposited or withdrawn; only one at the bank (or on hand) can bounce.
  • No step can be dated in the future, or before the step it follows.
  • Each step's journal can be reset only in reverse order: reset a clearance or bounce before the deposit, and the deposit before the receipt.
