Every rent invoice follows the same seven steps, from the contract to the money. What changes from one property to the next is whose rent it is and whose name is on the invoice. This topic walks through the steps once, then shows exactly what differs in each of the three cases.

Which case is yours

CaseHow the property is set upWhose rent, whose invoice
AThe company's own property Ownership type Owned. The rent is the company's revenue. The company invoices in its own name.
BA landlord's property, invoiced in the company's name Ownership type Managed, a Landlord and a Commission %. Invoice tenants in the owner's name is left unticked. The rent belongs to the landlord; the company earns its commission. The company invoices in its own name.
CA landlord's property, invoiced in the owner's name As B, with Invoice tenants in the owner's name ticked. The rent belongs to the landlord, and the landlord is the supplier on the invoice. The company issues it as agent.

All of it is set on the property, under Master Data ▸ Properties — see Properties. Landlords and their bank details are kept under Master Data ▸ Landlords. One company can hold all three kinds of property side by side.

A building that belongs to the company's owner personally

Owned means the company owns the building and its rent is the company's revenue. A building that belongs to a shareholder or a family member in their own name is not the company's: register it as Managed, with that person as the landlord, so their rent is kept apart from the company's income and they receive an owner statement like any other landlord (case B or C).

Decide before the first contract

The ownership type and the owner's-name option are read when each invoice is posted. Changing them later does not restate invoices already posted, and existing contracts keep the monthly income plan they were created with. Set them correctly before you create any contract under the property.

The seven steps, the same in every case

  1. Set up the property. Master Data ▸ Properties: ownership type and VAT category; for a managed property also the landlord, the commission %, Who holds the rent and whether tenants are invoiced in the owner's name. Then add its units.
  2. Create the contract. Master Data ▸ Leases. A new contract is a Draft with no invoices. When it is activated — or approved, if your company requires approval — the system creates every rent invoice of the term in one go: the annual rent divided by the invoices per year, one invoice for each billing date, numbered INV-<contract>-<year and month>, dated and due on the billing date. Each invoice starts as Draft and keeps the VAT treatment the contract had that day. See Lease Contracts.
  3. Post each invoice when it falls due. Operations ▸ Customer Invoices, then Post on its row. To post a whole month at once, open System Setup ▸ Closing Cockpit and select Issue invoices beside Rent invoices of the month issued. Posting writes the journal on the invoice date and gives a taxable invoice its tax invoice number. A draft invoice is not in the accounts, cannot be paid and is not shown to the tenant.
  4. Hand over the invoice. Tax Invoice (or Invoice, when no VAT is charged) on the row opens the document to print or save as PDF. Tenants with portal access see posted invoices there. Where e-invoicing applies, send it from the E-Invoice Monitor.
  5. Collect the money. Receive on the row records cash or a transfer — see Customer Receipts. Post-dated cheques are registered under PDC Receipts and settle the invoice when the bank clears them. The invoice moves to PartiallyPaid, then Paid.
  6. Recognise the month's income. System Setup ▸ Revenue Recognition, or Run recognition in the Closing Cockpit. For an owned property this releases one month of rent into revenue; for a managed property it takes one month of commission out of the landlord's money. See Revenue Recognition.
  7. Managed properties only: invoice the commission and pay the landlord. Operations ▸ Owner Settlements: Issue Commission Invoices each month, then Statement & Pay. See Owner Settlements.

Case A — the company's own property

  1. Property: Owned. No landlord, no commission.
  2. Activate the contract; the invoices appear as drafts under Operations ▸ Customer Invoices.
  3. Post the invoice on its date. A taxable invoice receives its TX- number.
  4. Print the tax invoice; send the e-invoice if the tenant is a business.
  5. Receive the payment or clear the cheque.
  6. Run revenue recognition every month.

Case B — a landlord's property, invoiced in the company's name

  1. Landlord: add them under Master Data ▸ Landlords with their TRN and bank account.
  2. Property: Managed, choose the Landlord, enter the Commission % and choose Who holds the rent. Leave Invoice tenants in the owner's name unticked.
  3. Activate the contract and Post each invoice on its date, exactly as in case A. The tenant's invoice looks the same as one for an owned property: the company is the supplier and the number is from the TX- series.
  4. Receive the payment or clear the cheque. The money lands where Who holds the rent says.
  5. Run revenue recognition every month: it moves that month's commission from the landlord's money into your revenue.
  6. After the month end, Operations ▸ Owner Settlements ▸ Issue Commission Invoices. Each landlord gets one tax invoice (CI-<year>-…, with a TX- tax invoice number) for the month's commission plus 5% VAT.
  7. Statement & Pay for the landlord: check the statement and pay the balance, in full or in part.

The VAT on the rent is the company's to pay in this case

Because the invoice is in the company's name, VAT charged on commercial rent goes to the company's VAT Payable and its VAT return — even though the rent itself is the landlord's. If the landlord is VAT registered and should declare that VAT themselves, use case C.

Case C — a landlord's property, invoiced in the owner's name

  1. Landlord and property as in case B, and tick Invoice tenants in the owner's name on the property. Record the landlord's TRN if they are VAT registered: with no TRN, no VAT is charged on the rent.
  2. Activate the contract and Post each invoice on its date. On posting, the invoice is stamped with the owner's name and TRN as they are that day, and a registered owner's invoice takes the next number in OTI-<property>-<year>-….
  3. Select Tax Invoice to print it. The landlord is shown as the supplier, "c/o" your company, with a note that paying the company settles the invoice. A landlord with no TRN gets a plain Invoice.
  4. There is nothing to send from the E-Invoice Monitor: the column shows Not required.
  5. Receive the payment or clear the cheque; the money lands where Who holds the rent says.
  6. Run revenue recognition, issue the monthly commission invoice and pay the landlord, as in case B. The owner statement shows an extra line, Add: VAT on rent invoiced in the owner's name, so the VAT you collected for them is paid over with the rent.
  7. Give the landlord their figures for the FTA from Reporting ▸ Owner VAT Return: their output tax on the rent, and as input tax the VAT on your commission and on costs recharged to them.

Correcting an invoice

  • Not yet posted: nothing is in the accounts. A wrong rent or date is a wrong contract — see Lease Contracts.
  • Posted, not sent as an e-invoice, nothing received against it: reset its journal to draft from Operations ▸ Journal Inquiry — see Draft, Post & Reset to Draft. The tax invoice number it was given stays with it.
  • Anything else: issue a credit note from the Credit Note button on its row.

Checking your work

  • Operations ▸ Customer Invoices: the GL column shows Posted or Not Posted for every invoice.
  • System Setup ▸ Closing Cockpit: Rent invoices of the month issued is red while any invoice dated in the month is unposted, and the period cannot be closed.
  • Reporting ▸ Tenant Statement: every invoice, receipt and credit note of one tenant.
  • Operations ▸ Owner Settlements: rent billed, commission and balance per landlord.
