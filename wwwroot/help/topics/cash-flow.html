The Statement of Cash Flows shows where cash came from and where it went over a period, prepared under IAS 7 using the direct method. It answers the question the Profit & Loss cannot: the business made a profit, so why is there less cash?

Running the report

  1. Enter the From and To dates. The default is the fiscal year to date.
  2. Refresh. Print gives a clean copy.

How to read it

Four cards summarise the period: Opening Cash, Operating cash flow, Net Movement and Closing Cash. The statement below splits the movement three ways:

SectionWhat lands there
OperatingRent collected, suppliers and expenses paid, VAT paid or refunded, deposits taken or returned — the day-to-day business.
InvestingBuying or selling long-term assets — property, fixed assets (accounts coded 15000 and above).
FinancingCapital put in by owners, owner drawings, loans — anything touching equity.

Each line is the account on the other side of the cash movement, with a Movements count of the journals behind it, so a surprising figure can be traced in Journal Inquiry.

The statement ends: net increase or decrease in cash + cash at the beginning = cash at the end.

What counts as cash

  • Included: the account mapped as Default Cash, plus every petty cash float.
  • Excluded: PDC On Hand. A post-dated cheque in the drawer has not been banked, so it is not a cash equivalent. It enters the statement when it clears.
  • Excluded: transfers between cash accounts. Topping up petty cash from the bank moves cash between pockets; it is not a cash flow.
It always reconciles

Every movement is read from the ledger itself, so the closing figure always agrees with the cash accounts in the Trial Balance. If it ever does not, a red warning appears at the top with the difference. That would mean a journal shape the report did not expect — report it through System Setup ▸ Send Feedback.

Nothing appears?

If the report says no cash accounts are configured, set the Default Cash mapping in Account Mapping. Like every report, it includes posted journals only.
